Sharding cost model · trn2.48xlarge · LNC = 2

How to spread one decider forward over NeuronCores

tools/cost_model.py estimates per-forward latency, MFU and instance throughput of Strands Decider 2B for every sharding strategy. It is calibrated against seven on-device measurements of the final (v3) kernels, validated on four held-out tensor-parallel measurements, and reads its collective costs from nightly nccom-test data scaled to in-graph cost.

1. Summary and decision

A Strands Decider request is a single prefill: one forward of T ≤ 4,096 tokens through a 1.373B-parameter non-embedding torso (18 Gated DeltaNet layers, 6 gated-attention layers, dense SwiGLU MLPs), with no decode loop. The weights (3.78 GB in bf16, embedding table included) fit in one logical core's 24 GiB many times over. Every sharding strategy is therefore optional: it can only trade instance throughput for single-request latency.

The cost model puts numbers on that trade. It is calibrated on device to the final (v3) kernels: seven single-core points fit within 3%, and in-graph collectives cost 3.1× the nccom-test figures. Four tensor-parallel measurements were held out of the fit, and the model predicts them within 2.5–12.6%.

It finds that no strategy with P > 1 matches data parallelism's instance throughput; pipeline parallelism only ties it, and gives no latency gain. For long prompts it ranks CP2 with the two-pass GDN state correction best: 81.5 ms and 44.0% MFU at T = 4,096, against 91.1 ms modelled for TP2 (87.9 ms measured). CP's only traffic is the K/V and GDN-state all-gathers, 1.9 ms per forward. TP2 moves its activations through 48 in-graph all-reduces, 19.2 ms per forward.

Decision
  • Default: data parallelism, one model replica per LNC2 core (64 per instance), for requests up to the 2,816-token bucket. Measured: 1.71M tokens/s per instance at 46.0% MFU (64 replicas, T = 2,048).
  • Long requests today: TP2 inside one chip (implemented and measured), on adjacent logical cores. This covers requests over ~2.8K tokens, and multi-question requests whose state is longer than ~2K tokens. Measured at T = 4,096: 87.9 ms against 133.6 ms on one core, at 40.8% MFU over the two cores.
  • Next implementation step: CP2 with the two-pass GDN state correction. The calibrated model gives 81.5 ms and 44.0% MFU at T = 4,096, and 83.5% of DP's instance throughput (TP2: 74.6%). It is not yet implemented, so this prediction is unvalidated, and it depends on the in-graph collective cost (§7.2).
  • Not recommended:
    • TSP: CP's data flow plus weight streaming that is exposed at short T. Its memory saving buys nothing at 3.78 GB of weights.
    • Ulysses and the hybrid: 16–19 ms of in-graph all-to-all per forward at P = 2.
    • PP: no latency gain.
    • TP4 and TP8: at most 58% of DP's instance throughput.
  • EP is not applicable: every layer has a dense SwiGLU MLP; there are no experts.

2. What the model computes

estimate(strategy, P, T, B) sums, layer by layer, the time of each op class on one rank of a P-rank replica. It assumes no overlap between compute and communication (overlap = 0, which the in-graph measurements in §5.2 support):

# per layer, on one rank of a P-rank replica GEMM = max(FLOPs_rank / (peak × eff(T_rank)), weight_bytes_rank / HBM_BW) GDN core = (gdn_fixed_us + ns_per_head_token × heads_rank × T × B) / lnc_speedup attn core = causal FLOPs_rank / (peak × attn_eff) # 4·8·256·(T·(ctx−T) + T²/2) elementwise = bytes_per_token_layer × tokens_rank / HBM_BW + per_layer_overhead collectives = Σ coll_us(op, bytes, P) × collective_scale # nccom data × 3.1 (in graph) # per forward latency = Σ layers + embedding gather + head MFU = model FLOPs / (latency × peak × P) instance tok/s = (64 / P) × B × T / latency # PP: × P (stages pipeline requests)

A strategy is infeasible if a rank's weights exceed 80% of its 24 GiB of HBM. None does: the full model is 3.78 GB. For the collectives, coll_us interpolates the measured latency table log-log between 64 KiB and 32 MiB, falls linearly toward the small-message floor below 64 KiB, and extrapolates linearly above 32 MiB. Rank counts are rounded up to 4, 8 or 16. P = 2 uses the 4-rank intra-chip numbers, because no 2-rank trn2 LNC2 data exists. collective_scale = IN_GRAPH_COLLECTIVE_SCALE = 3.1 then converts nccom-test latency to in-graph cost. That is the all-reduce ratio at 16 MiB (§5.2), applied to every op and size.

3. FLOP and byte accounting per op class

The model uses the same FLOP definition as the MFU figures in results/summary.md: 2 FLOPs per multiply-add over the non-embedding matmuls, causal attention (QKᵀ + PV), and the GDN chunk core at C = 128, which is 2.871 GFLOP per token at T = 2,048.

* The model gives the GDN core its own measured cost law instead of a roofline (§4). As a rough guide, its HBM traffic per token per layer is q, k, v and the output in bf16 plus g and β in fp32, about 16.5 KB, so its intensity is about 250 FLOP per byte.

Ridge point. With 166.75 TF/s and 725 GB/s per core, the roofline ridge is 230 FLOP per byte (221 with the profiler's 158 TF/s and 716 GB/s). Every projection and MLP GEMM reads its weights once per forward, so its intensity equals the tokens in the forward on that rank. A rank therefore becomes compute-bound at about 230 tokens. Below that, a forward streams 2.75 GB of weights (3.8 ms at 725 GB/s) whatever its size. Sharding weights (TP, TSP) keeps each rank's GEMMs at T tokens, so their efficiency is unchanged. Splitting tokens (CP, Ulysses) pushes each rank's GEMMs toward the ridge, where the calibrated efficiency drops (0.45 at 256 tokens against 0.86 at 2,048). That is why CP and Ulysses lose most at small T: at T = 256, CP2 (14.0 ms) is slower than one core (13.6 ms). The elementwise term is the other large cost. The fit puts it at 250 KB per token per layer (§4), 25% of the single-core forward at T = 4,096.

5. Collectives

5.1 nccom-test data (the model's input)

The latency tables come from nightly nccom-test runs. The 4-, 8- and 16-rank AllReduce, ReduceScatter, AllGather and AllToAll curves come from single-group runs on 1, 2 and 4 chips, from the 2026-11-10 nightly. The 32- and 64-rank AllReduce points come from the 2026-10-23 nightly. Sizes are the maximum buffer: the AG output or the RS input.

  • Inside one chip (4 ranks) is fastest for every op. AR reaches 200 GB/s busbw at 32 MiB (252 µs). The model's P = 2 configurations use these numbers.
  • 8 ranks (2 chips) is the worst AR/RS/AG shape at large sizes: AR 16 MiB takes 267.8 µs, against 202.9 µs at 16 ranks, where the runtime switches to the Kangaring algorithm.
  • AllToAll degrades steeply with ranks: 16 MiB takes 140.4 µs at 4 ranks, 396.4 at 8 and 586.5 at 16 (busbw 89.6 → 37.0 → 26.8 GB/s). This is what limits Ulysses beyond one chip.
  • Concurrency: 16 concurrent 4-rank AllGather groups match a single group within 1–2% at every size, so intra-chip groups do not contend. There is no concurrent data for 8- or 16-rank groups, which share inter-chip links.
  • Gaps: no 2-rank suite has ever reported (w64_g2_s2 is defined but dark), and there is no 16-rank contiguous-group suite. The bot also reports AllToAllV for 16 × 4-rank groups (…_a2av_4r_intrachip_tp4: 280.8 µs at 32 MiB) and for 8 × 8-rank groups across 2 chips (…_a2av_8r_2chip_tp8: 1,763 µs at 16 MiB, its ceiling).

5.2 In-graph collectives on the native backend

The decider does not call nccom. Its collectives are traced into the compiled NEFF with torch.distributed._functional_collectives and run by the runtime inside the forward. neuron/bench_cc.py times a chain of 8 collectives in one graph on [T, 2048] bf16 at world 2 (cores 0–1), and subtracts a chain of scales:

  • Superlinear. Doubling the buffer from 4 to 8 MiB triples the in-graph all-reduce (72 → 213 µs). The nccom curve grows sublinearly over the same range (41.7 → 70.7 µs).
  • Not overlapped. In the TP2 forward at T = 4,096, removing the 48 all-reduces (a timing-only experiment) cuts latency from 93.2 to 71.7 ms. That 21.5 ms is close to 48 × 402 µs = 19.3 ms, so the collectives sit on the critical path. Splitting each layer's tail into 4 token chunks, which gives the scheduler independent chains, recovers ~6 ms (93.6 → 87.9 ms against 2 chunks).
  • Per-call cost. A graph that holds a single collective costs 130–230 µs more than a non-collective graph (docs/native_stack_howto.md §3: 188 µs per call against 72 µs in-graph for 4 MiB at world 2). Collectives must stay inside the model graph.
  • The consequence for the model. The calibrated model applies the 3.1× factor to every collective. It then predicts 19.2 ms of TP2 communication at T = 4,096, inside the 16–21 ms measured on device. One factor for every op and size is a simplification: at 4 MiB the measured ratio is 1.7×, and in-graph AG and A2A were never measured separately.

DP

One replica per logical core, with no process group and no collectives. Single-request latency is the single-core latency. Instance throughput is 64 × the per-core rate, minus contention: measured 46.0% MFU per core with 64 replicas, against 52.8% alone.

TP / TP + SP

Column-shard the input projections (GDN q|k|v|z|b|a by head; attention q, gate and k/v by head; MLP gate/up by column). Row-shard the output projections, then all-reduce the partial sums. GDN state and conv state are per head, so they stay local, and the shared-prefix path needs no extra communication. SP replaces each AR with RS + AG and shards norms and residuals over tokens. KV heads (2) are replicated when P > 2. Attention caps P at 8.

CP: two-pass GDN state correction

The delta rule is affine in the state: a block of tokens maps an incoming state S to S·M + S_local. Each rank runs its T/P tokens twice: a local pass from S = 0 gives the outputs and S_local, and a transition pass with v = 0 from S = I gives M. An all-gather of (S_local, M), a prefix composition and a correction matmul then fix every rank's outputs. The GDN core costs 2 × core(T/P), so CP buys no GDN speedup. K/V are all-gathered for attention (GQA keeps them small). These two all-gathers are CP’s only traffic.

Ulysses / hybrid

Projections and MLP run on T/P tokens. An all-to-all re-shards q, k, v, g and β from tokens to heads (16/P GDN heads over the full sequence), so the recurrence runs unsplit, and a second all-to-all returns the outputs. Attention re-shards the same way (8 query heads, so P ≤ 8). The hybrid keeps Ulysses for GDN and uses the CP K/V all-gather for attention.

TSP

"Folding Tensor and Sequence Parallelism for Memory-Efficient Transformer Training & Inference" (Shyam, Golubeva, Anthony; arXiv:2604.26294, April 2026). Each rank holds a weight shard and a sequence shard. For attention, ranks iterate over broadcast parameter shards and exchange K/V; for gated MLPs, weight shards circulate in a ring. It moves weights instead of activations to save memory. Here the replicated weights (3.78 GB) already fit in 24 GiB, so that saving buys nothing, and the data flow is CP's, including the two GDN passes.

PP

Layers split into P stages. One request still crosses every stage in sequence, so its latency is DP's plus the hops. With requests pipelined, throughput at best equals DP on the same cores.

7. Predictions vs measurements

7.1 DP, TP2 and TP4 against the device

Table view (model and measured)

The DP curve passes within 3% of every fit target. The DP buckets that were not fit targets land within 8%: T = 256 is the worst at −7.7%, and 768, 1,536, 3,072 and 3,584 are within 5%. For tensor parallelism the model predicts 46.6 / 91.1 ms (TP2 at 2,048 / 4,096) against 47.8 / 87.9 ms measured, and 30.7 / 59.0 ms (TP4) against 27.3 / 56.9 ms. Measured MFU falls from 52.8% (DP, one core) to 36.9–40.8% (TP2) and 31.6–32.3% (TP4), close to the modelled 37.8–39.4% and 28.7–30.4%. The 64-replica DP point (1.71M tokens/s) sits 12% below the DP throughput line. The model has no inter-replica contention, and the measured per-replica time under load is 76.7 ms per forward, against 66.8 ms pipelined alone.

Sensitivity: nccom collectives

With raw nccom-test collective costs (select "nccom collectives" above), the P = 2 ranking at T = 4,096 inverts: HYB2 74.7, TPSP2 75.4, ULY2 75.5, TP2 78.1, CP2 = TSP2 80.2 ms. CP2's lead exists only because in-graph collectives are slow. That is what the held-out TP points support (§4), but CP2's prediction itself is unvalidated until it is built.

7.3 Where the time goes at T = 4,096, P = 2

Every P = 2 strategy halves the GEMM time (76.6 → 38–39 ms). CP and TSP leave the GDN core at 20.3 ms, slightly above the single core's 19.2 ms, because each runs two passes on T/2 tokens and pays the fixed per-call cost twice. In return they pay only 1.9 ms of communication. TP, Ulysses and the hybrid halve the GDN core (10.1 ms) but pay 16.4–21.4 ms of in-graph collectives. At the calibrated collective cost, the communication saving outweighs the GDN saving.

8. The decision and why

  1. Throughput belongs to DP. The model and the device agree. 64 independent single-core replicas reach 1.71M tokens/s (46.0% MFU per core) at T = 2,048, and 1.73M with 512 × 4 batches. Every sharded strategy delivers less per instance: at T = 4,096, CP2 83.5%, TP2 74.6%, TP4 57.6% and ULY8 48.4% of DP in the calibrated model. JevBench's lengths (p50 178, p90 2,471, p99 3,072 tokens) put 96.6% of requests on the DP pool, at ≤ 2,816 tokens (measured).
  2. Long requests today: TP2 within a chip. It is implemented and measured. Above the 2,816-token DP bucket one core takes 94–134 ms, and TP2 on two adjacent logical cores cuts T = 4,096 to 87.9 ms. It also cuts the 5-question request with a 2,500-token state from 113.3 to 81.6 ms. TP keeps the GDN and conv states local to each rank, so the shared-prefix path needs no state exchange. TP4 goes further (56.9 ms) but at 31.6% MFU.
  3. Next: CP2 with the two-pass GDN state correction. The calibrated model predicts 81.5 ms and 44.0% MFU at T = 4,096: 7% under TP2's measured 87.9 ms, and 11% under TP2's modelled 91.1 ms. Its traffic is only a 4 MiB state all-gather per GDN layer and an 8 MiB K/V all-gather per attention layer, 1.9 ms per forward. In exchange it must run the GDN core twice on T/2 tokens (no GDN speedup), add the prefix composition and correction matmul, balance causal attention across ranks (zigzag), and feed the composed state into the shared-prefix path. The prediction rests on all-gathers that were never measured in graph and on the 3.1× scale: under nccom costs CP2 ranks last at P = 2. Measuring in-graph all-gather at 4–8 MiB is therefore the first step of the implementation.
  4. Why not TSP. At long T it is CP plus a weight stream (1.37 GB per forward per rank) that the GEMMs only hide from about T = 3,584. Its memory saving buys nothing: 3.78 GB of weights against 24 GiB per core.
  5. Why not Ulysses or the hybrid. They need an all-to-all on each side of every mixer: 48 per forward, 16–19 ms at P = 2 with in-graph cost. There is no 2-rank or in-graph A2A data. Their advantage only appears at P = 8 (35.2 ms), where throughput falls to 48% of DP and the all-to-alls cross chips.
  6. The model is small. At 2.87 GFLOP per token, a 4,096-token forward is 133.6 ms on one core. The latency problem is mild, so the right answer spends the fewest extra cores on it.

9. Limits of the model

  • It is calibrated to seven single-core v3 points, and validated on four TP points (2.5–12.6%). Its CP, Ulysses, hybrid and TSP predictions are not validated by any measurement.
  • One collective scale (3.1×, from all-reduce at 16 MiB) is applied to every op and size. The measured all-reduce ratio is 1.7× at 4 MiB, so CP's 4–8 MiB all-gathers may be over- or under-costed. In-graph AG, RS and A2A were measured only as RS + AG at world 2.
  • P = 2 uses 4-rank nccom data, because no 2-rank suite has reported.
  • Elementwise traffic is a single fitted constant (250 KB per token per layer) that every strategy shards by its token or head split. The real split varies by op.
  • Attention efficiency is a constant (0.40), and the CP attention core assumes zigzag load balance.
  • TSP weight streaming is assumed hidden behind 90% of the GEMM time.
  • There is no inter-replica contention: 64 measured replicas run 12% below the model.
  • There is no host, tokenisation or batching-queue time. End-to-end request latencies are in the optimization report.
